A Six Pack Isn't Unattainable. It Just Takes a Little Forethought.

By Jake Murillo · November 24, 2026

It's 5 AM, I'm in my kitchen making food, and I'm thinking about something people say all the time.

They see someone with a six pack and go, "Oh man, that physique is so unattainable. That's so extreme."

Here's the thing. You're looking at the result, not the ordinary habits that built it. The physique gets the comments. The routine does the work.

And a lot of people call it unrealistic before they've ever wholeheartedly committed to it. They convince themselves it's out of reach, so they never see a reason to try. I genuinely believe people are capable of far more than they give themselves credit for. Your journey won't look the same as anyone else's, take the same amount of time, or need the same approach. But don't disqualify yourself before you've given yourself a real shot. The question is whether you're willing to commit to the process, stick to the plan, and adjust along the way.

What actually goes into visible abs

I don't want to oversimplify this. Whether your abs show, and how easily, depends on your genetics, where your body stores fat, how developed your abdominal muscles are, and how you eat and train. Some people see definition sooner than others. That's real.

So I'm not saying a six pack is equally easy for everyone. I'm saying the process doesn't have to be complicated. Most people don't need a secret. They need a plan they follow.

Have a plan and stick to it

In my TikTok, I said you just have to be consistent with making your breakfast every morning and your lunch every day. That was my example because that's what I was doing at 5 AM. But it applies to dinner, your pre-workout meal, or a high-protein snack too.

And it doesn't have to be a traditional breakfast, lunch, or dinner. What matters is that your food fits within your calorie range for your goal right now. Everyone's range is different, which is why there's real nuance in how much of this and how much of that you should eat.

Leave a meal to "I'll figure it out later" and you end up at a drive-thru. Later is when most plans fall apart. You're asking willpower to do a job that planning should have already done.

Weekends: flexible, not a free-for-all

A physique is not a prison sentence. You can go out, enjoy a meal, and still make progress.

But flexibility works best inside some structure. Your results come from your overall intake and consistency across the whole week, not from any single meal. One dinner out won't wreck you. Ignoring your plan every weekend can undo the week's progress. The goal is a way of eating you can live with seven days a week, not strict weekdays and anything-goes weekends.

Move more, on purpose

Move your body more than you have been. That might be walks, strength training, or breaking up the all-day sit. If you already train, it means showing up on the days that aren't convenient.

What forethought looks like tonight

People want to say it's rocket science. It's not. It just takes a little forethought. Here's what that looks like, whatever your experience level:

  1. Decide what you're eating tomorrow. Write it down tonight. Don't wait until you're hungry.
  2. Prep something ahead. Even one meal or a high-protein snack ready to grab makes the day easier.
  3. Check it against your goal. Does tomorrow's food roughly fit the calories and protein you're aiming for?
  4. Put your movement on the calendar. Pick the time for your workout or walk, the same way you'd schedule anything else that matters.

That's forethought over willpower. You do the thinking once, then you just follow through.

Getting there vs. staying there

Reaching a physique and keeping it are two different skills. Holding visible abs year-round isn't automatically unrealistic. The goal is to reach a level of body fat that's sustainable for you, and to know how to stay there.

Getting older doesn't automatically mean gaining fat. What usually happens is life changes: your activity, your energy needs, your responsibilities, your eating habits. But the nutrition and movement don't get adjusted to match. That's why understanding your needs, having a plan, and knowing when to change it matter more over time. Forethought isn't just how you get results. It's how you keep them.
